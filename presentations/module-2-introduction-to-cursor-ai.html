01 — What it is

Same editor shape, a different loop inside it

A traditional IDE is a human-driven loop: you write code, a compiler or runtime tells you what happened, you read the output and fix it yourself. Cursor keeps that same editor shape but closes the loop differently — your intent and an index of your actual codebase both feed a model, which proposes a change for you to review and approve. It's still your codebase and your decision on every diff; what changed is who drafts the first pass.

02 — Positioning

Not "Copilot, but different"

Placing AI coding tools on two axes makes the real distinction visible. One axis is where the tool lives — a separate chat window with no view of your repository, or built into the IDE itself. The other is how far its autonomy goes — a single-line autocomplete, or a full agentic workflow that plans, edits multiple files, and runs your terminal. Copilot made autocomplete-in-the-IDE mainstream; Cursor's ground floor is the full agentic corner of this map.

03 — Footprint

Bigger than the code you're typing

A classic AI coding assistant earns its keep in one lifecycle stage: Development. Used as an agentic engineering platform — the way this course treats it — Cursor's footprint stretches across the full SDLC: requirements exploration, design and spec work, development, testing, review, deployment, and maintenance. Module 9 onward is where that wider footprint gets hands-on.

04 — Execution modes

Watched, or delegated

Local execution keeps the agent on your machine, in your editor, working in a tight synchronous loop you watch step by step — the mode you'll use for most of this course. Background/delegated execution hands a whole task to an agent running on separate compute; it works independently while you do something else, and you come back to review a finished result. Same underlying agent, two different trust models.

05 — Subtopic: positioning within Module 1

Where Cursor sits on the maturity curve

Module 1 introduced a four-stage landscape: prompt-driven coding, context engineering, agents, and multi-agent orchestration. Cursor isn't parked at one point on that curve — its native capabilities span from context engineering through agents, and the subagent/orchestration modules later in this course push you into the fourth stage using the same platform.
